Northern Arakan · autumn 2017

What the satellites recorded

In the autumn of 2017, satellites passing over northern Arakan photographed Rohingya villages, and analysts at the United Nations mapped where buildings had been destroyed. This is what that record shows for the villages in this archive, and what it cannot show.

The archive

1,023 villages, each with a name

Every dot is a Rohingya village in one of four townships of Arakan, the land Myanmar calls Rakhine State: Maungdaw, Buthidaung, Rathedaung and Sittwe.

FARA keeps each village's name as its people say it, beside its official name and its name in Burmese. Fará is the Rohingya word for village.

August 2017

The villages emptied

From late August 2017, more than 750,000 Rohingya fled to Bangladesh, according to the UN refugee agency. Most came from the three northern townships on this map.

What happened to the places they left was recorded from above.

The survey

1,862destroyed zones
mapped by UNOSAT

UNOSAT studied satellite images of Maungdaw, Buthidaung and Rathedaung taken between 31 August and 11 October 2017, and outlined every zone where it found buildings destroyed.

It mapped 1,862 zones, about 3,170 hectares in all. This is Maungdaw township, where most of them lie.

A second witness

164villages with a fire
detected within a kilometre

UNOSAT read the damage from pictures. Other satellites, built to watch for heat, recorded the fires themselves, each with its day.

Between 25 August and 30 November 2017, NASA’s fire-detecting satellites recorded fire within a kilometre of 164 villages in this archive. Counting only the sharper of the two instruments, it is 149 villages. In the same weeks of every other year from 2013 to 2019, that instrument never found more than 10.

A detection is heat seen from orbit. It does not say what burned, and in the monsoon many fires burn unseen under cloud.

What it cannot show

“None detected” is not “still standing”

At 596 villages UNOSAT marked no destroyed zone within 500 metres. That is not proof those villages stand.

The imagery ends on 11 October 2017. Parts of the area were under cloud. Destruction went on after that date, and UNOSAT did not check its findings on the ground.

Sittwe township, with 93 villages in this archive, lay outside the survey altogether.

The names remain

Every village had a name

A village is more than its buildings. Its name carries the memory of the people who lived there, and a name is the easiest thing to erase: by renaming a place, or by leaving it off the map.

FARA keeps the names. If you know one this archive is missing or has wrong, tell us.

Sources and method

  • UNOSAT: Destroyed areas in Buthidaung, Maungdaw, and Rathedaung Townships of Rakhine State in Myanmar — the destroyed zones and their dates. CC BY-SA. Analysis from satellite imagery, not validated on the ground.
  • NASA FIRMS: active fire detections (VIIRS 375 m and MODIS 1 km) — the fires and their days. A detection within 1 km of a village's recorded position is counted for that village.
  • Esri World Imagery Wayback — the two satellite pictures and their capture dates.
  • UNHCR: Rohingya emergency — the number of people who fled to Bangladesh from August 2017.
  • Rohingya Khobor: Arakan Maps Project — village names and positions.
  • UN OCHA: Myanmar Subnational Administrative Boundaries (COD-AB) — township boundaries. CC BY-IGO.

How villages were matched: a village is counted as destroyed when a UNOSAT zone covers its recorded position or at least one hectare is marked destroyed within 300 metres of it, and as “nearby” when any zone lies within 500 metres. Matching is by position only and has not been reviewed village by village. The per-village results are in status_unosat_2017.csv.
